# Gas Day: narration script

2:40 in all. Written by `film/render.py` from `film/scenes.js`, filmed on flow data to 2026-06-30. Voice: Kokoro `af_heart` at 0.97 speed. To replace a line with a real recording, save it as `film/out/vo/recorded/<id>.wav`.

## 0:00 Beneath your feet

**Shows:** The flow map on its own, full screen, on the latest gas day.

**Camera:** Fades up from black high over Canada, then pushes in and tilts along the network.

**Sound:** A low pad fades in under the first line.

`0:01` `open-1` Right now, natural gas is moving beneath your feet. Across mountains, prairie and shield, through steel you will never see.

`0:09` `open-2` This is twenty years of that movement, drawn from public records, on a single map.

## 0:16 How to read the map

**Shows:** The whole application: map, legend, time bar and chart panel. Line width, colour and pulses.

**Camera:** Levels out as the app slides in around the map, then flies west into Alberta.

**Sound:** The pad opens up. A soft pulse starts.

`0:18` `read-1` This is Gas Day. Every line follows a route on TC Energy's Canadian network, and every number comes from flow data filed with the Canada Energy Regulator.

`0:29` `read-2` Thick lines carry more gas. Brighter blue means a route is closer to full. And the pulses show which way the gas is flowing.

## 0:38 Twenty years in twenty seconds

**Shows:** The time slider playing from 2006 to the latest gas day, with the throughput chart following.

**Camera:** Pulls back to the whole network and holds while the years run.

**Sound:** The pulse quickens and the pad lifts through the time-lapse.

`0:39` `lapse-1` Press play, and twenty years run past in seconds. More than seven thousand days of flow.

`0:45` `lapse-2` Watch the west. In 2010, about five billion cubic feet a day came down this corridor, out of Alberta and British Columbia.

`0:54` `lapse-3` By 2025, it was more than twelve.

## 0:58 The pipe that changed direction

**Shows:** Clicking a key point. The pulses at Niagara reverse between 2007 and 2025. The net exports chart.

**Camera:** Dives from the whole network to the Niagara River, then lifts over southern Ontario.

**Sound:** The pulse drops out for the reveal, then returns.

`1:00` `niagara-1` Click any point, and the panel tells its story. This is Niagara, on the border with New York.

`1:06` `niagara-2` In 2007, gas flowed east here, out of Canada, at around eight hundred million cubic feet a day.

`1:13` `niagara-3` Then American shale changed everything. Watch the pulses. By 2025, the same pipe was carrying gas the other way, into Ontario.

`1:23` `niagara-4` One chart shows the whole border shifting, region by region.

## 1:28 The coldest week

**Shows:** January 2024 at the Calgary key point: the headline numbers, the weather scatter and the utilization grid.

**Camera:** Crosses the continent to Calgary and settles low over the city.

**Sound:** The pad darkens and thins. A cold, high tone.

`1:30` `cold-1` January 2024. An Arctic front settles over the prairies, and Calgary's average temperature falls to minus thirty-four.

`1:39` `cold-2` Gas delivered toward the city jumps by more than half in a week, to the highest level this point has ever recorded.

`1:46` `cold-3` Here, every dot is one day. The colder it gets, the further right it sits, and the more gas this point moves.

`1:53` `cold-4` And one grid shows, month by month, which points are running close to full.

## 1:58 Fire season

**Shows:** The wildfire layer: burned areas for the slider year, live satellite hotspots, and the burned-area chart.

**Camera:** Rises north over the boreal forest, tilted, and drifts east across the 2023 burn scars.

**Sound:** A low swell as the burned areas appear.

`2:00` `fire-1` Now switch on the wildfire layer, and go back to the summer of 2023. The worst fire season Canada has ever recorded.

`2:09` `fire-2` Each orange shape is a large fire. By this map's estimate, more than half a million hectares burned within fifty kilometres of these routes that year.

`2:19` `fire-3` And the red dots are live: satellite hotspots from the last twenty-four hours.

## 2:24 Only public data

**Shows:** The map alone again on the latest gas day, then the end card with the data sources.

**Camera:** The app slides away and the camera pulls back to the whole network.

**Sound:** The pad resolves and fades.

`2:26` `close-1` No private feeds. No licences. Only public data, a time slider, and a new way to see a network that never stops.

`2:35` `close-2` This is Gas Day.
